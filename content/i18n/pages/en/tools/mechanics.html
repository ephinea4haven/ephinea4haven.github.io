This page summarizes the core formulas of Ephinea PSOBB. Unless noted otherwise, formulas and values come from the Ephinea Wiki’s Game mechanics page. The game truncates final damage rather than rounding it: a result of 100.92 deals 100.

A: Physical damage formula

Physical damage is all damage except fire- and lightning-type specials and techniques. The ATP shown in brackets in the menu is the class’s maximum; the game actually uses a hidden base ATP and adds a class-dependent random variance on every attack.

Ranger4

Step 1: Equipment ATP (EQATP)

EQATP = [WeaponATP_min + (grind × 2) + frame ATP + barrier ATP] × [weapon attribute% + 1]

  • WeaponATP_min = the low end of the weapon’s ATP range
  • Weapon attribute% = the attribute percentage that applies to the target type (Native / A.Beast / Machine / Dark)

Step 2: Base ATP

BaseATP = BaseATP_max - Pvar_max

  • BaseATP_max = the ATP shown in brackets in the menu

Step 3: Effective ATP

ATPeff = [BaseATP + (Wvar × WSpread)] × (1 + Shifta%) + EQATP + Pvar

  • Wvar = a random decimal from 0 to 1 on each attack
  • WSpread = the width of the weapon’s ATP range (100 for a 300–400 ATP weapon)
  • Shifta% = the boost percentage of the current Shifta level (see Section D)
  • Pvar = a random class variance on each attack: Hunter 1–6, Ranger 1–4, Force 1–3

Shifta only applies to base ATP and the weapon’s random range, not to EQATP. The attribute percentage only multiplies the weapon’s minimum ATP and leaves the random range unchanged: a 300–400 ATP weapon with 100% attribute has 600–700 ATP, not 600–800.

Step 4: Effective DFP

DFPeff = DFP_base × (1 - Zalure%)

Step 5: Final damage

Damage = [(ATPeff - DFPeff) / 5] × 0.9 × attack multiplier

Criticals and LCK

  • Player critical rate: min(LCK, 100) / 5 %. Characters cap at 100 LCK, so the highest critical rate is 20%.
  • Enemy critical rate: min(LCK, 100) / 2 %.
  • Critical damage: attack result × 1.5.
  • Only attacks that have already hit, and are not techniques or fixed damage, roll for a critical.
  • LCK is the only non-resistance stat that does not grow with levels; it can only be raised by equipment and .

Two different kinds of Luck: the LCK stat only affects criticals, not drop rates; Ephinea also has a separate daily forecast, which gives a rare drop rate bonus to characters matching the day’s class, race or sex. /forecast shows the day’s forecast conditions, and /luck shows your current character’s forecast level. See the Ephinea Wiki on LCK and the daily forecast rules.

B: Hit calculation when players attack enemies

In this section the player attacks and the enemy defends. The player’s ATA and the enemy’s EVP decide whether an attack hits; a hit then goes on to the physical damage calculation in Section A. Techniques have no hit check: they always take effect when they touch the target.

Step 1: Total accuracy (ATA)

ATA_total = ATA_base + weapon ATA + frame ATA + barrier ATA

  • ATA_base = the ATA shown in brackets in the menu
  • Weapon ATA includes the weapon’s Hit attribute
  • ATA_total is the ATA shown outside the brackets in the menu; you can read it directly

Step 2: Effective accuracy

ATA_eff = ATA_total × attack type modifier × combo step modifier

EVP_eff = EVP_base × status modifier

Step 4: Final hit rate

Melee hit rate (%) = ATA_eff - (EVP_eff × 0.2)

Ranged hit rate (%) = ATA_eff - (EVP_eff × 0.2) - distance penalty

  • The result is used directly as a percentage: 100 or more always hits, and a negative value always misses.
  • Distance penalty = distance × 0.33, and it only applies to Hunters and Forces without ; Rangers and characters with Smartlink have no distance penalty.
  • Bullet-type shots stop checking for hits once the target has moved more than 8 units; if a knockback makes the shot vanish early, no MISS is shown.

Enemy hit rate against players

SLW’s measurements on Ephinea fit this approximation:

Enemy hit rate (%) ≈ clamp(enemy ATA - player EVP × 0.2, 0, 100)

It can also be written as enemy ATA - player EVP / 5, the same form as the formula for players attacking enemies in Section B. For example, against an enemy with 290 ATA, 1450 EVP gives a theoretical hit rate of 0%, and 1442 EVP gives 1.6%. In the video, about 300 attacks were all evaded at 1457 EVP, and hits were seen after dropping to 1442.

Scope and reading the tables

  • This order and hit-rate relation only apply to attacks that roll for evasion. Fixed damage, missiles, beams, stomps and other special attacks may not be evadable with EVP.
  • Use the enemy’s actual ATA in the current difficulty, mode and quest; the base value in a data table is not always the effective value in battle.
  • Zero damage does not mean no hit reaction: a successful evade does not move you back, while Guard pushes you back, which affects your positioning and the risk of taking repeated hits.

D: Technique damage formula

Damage = [(MST + technique base power) / 5] × (1 + class boost + weapon boost + frame boost + barrier boost) × (1 - target resistance)

  • Technique damage depends on MST and is reduced by the target’s matching resistance, not by its DFP.
  • Techniques have no hit check and never crit.
  • All percentage boosts are added first and then multiplied: a FOnewearl with equipped has a Foie boost of +30% + 30% = +60%.
  • Megid boosts do not raise the instant-kill rate; they make it pierce instead, and multiple Megid boosts do not stack.
  • Jellen and Zalure have no class boosts; Reverser and Ryuker have no boosts at all. For equipment boosts see Equipment technique boosts.

Megid instant-kill rate

Megid deals no damage; its base power is the instant-kill chance in percent, reduced by the target’s EDK:

Instant-kill rate (%) = technique base power - target EDK

For example, Lv.30 Megid (base power 100) against a target with 80 EDK has a 20% instant-kill rate.

Resta healing

Healing = MST × 0.5 + technique base power

The Mag Photon Blast Leilla’s healing also has Mag IQ, chain and donation modifiers; see healing and support PBs in Section G.

E: Knockdown

In the general hit path of the locally saved Ephinea client, the knockdown flag is set when the internal damage > max HP × 25% . The comparison happens before the damage is truncated to an integer.

Why can 250 displayed damage still knock you down?Internal damage of 250.5 is already over the threshold, but the integer damage shown and taken is 250. So the displayed damage alone cannot tell you whether the threshold was crossed.

Threshold source and scope

Ephinea Wiki summarizes this as “25% or more”. The strict boundary here follows the locally saved Ephinea client: try_knockdown_player sets the knockdown flag when internal damage is strictly greater than 25% of max HP.

This chart explains the general damage threshold and does not cover knockdowns, deaths or other states set directly by special attacks. It was checked against a local client snapshot; whether the live runtime adds further changes has not been verified.

G: Mag Photon Blasts (PB): effects, damage and charging

The six PBs are four attacks, one heal and one support. First see how each works, what affects it and when to use it, then look up the damage and chain calculations. All four attack PBs deal light damage, reduced by the target’s ELT (light resistance).

The six PBs: effects and when to use them

A single PB uses your own stats; in a chain, every stat below comes from the first character in the chain and their Mag as seen by the current client. Q is the PB amount and N the number of PBs in the effective chain; see the parameter table below.

Serpent Farlla

How it works
An area attack on the enemies around you, suited to several targets crowding in.
What affects it
Base power uses Synchro, ATP and IQ; MST is added after the chain and donation modifiers. The higher the Synchro, the more raising ATP pays off.
When to use
With the same stats and light resistance, Pilla has higher base power. Farlla works against enemies around you when it is what you have; when comparing area PBs, also count how many targets each actually covers.
In a chain
Even with low damage of its own, joining as a different PB from the previous one still raises N, strengthening the attacks, heals and Twins support across the chain.

Base power Btrunc(Synchro × ATP × 0.025 + IQ × 2.5 + 500)

Dolphin Estlla

How it works
Attacks in a straight line in front of the character and can hit several enemies along the path. Enemies off to the sides do not count as covered just because they are nearby.
What affects it
Among the four attack PBs, only Estlla uses EVP instead of ATP. Synchro and IQ affect base power, and MST is still added after the chain modifier.
When to use
Use it when enemies line up; mind your position and facing before firing. Low ATP does not make Estlla weak; compare it using EVP, not by weapon attack power.
In a chain
If Estlla is meant to carry the damage, the first character’s EVP matters; later Estllas also use the first character’s stats. It can alternate with area PBs.

Base power Btrunc(Synchro × EVP × 0.15 + IQ × 15 + 350)

Stag Golla

How it works
A single-target attack that focuses on one enemy and does not clear groups.
What affects it
Uses Synchro, ATP, IQ and MST; its base power formula is exactly the same as Pilla’s.
When to use
With only one target to deal with, judge it by single-target damage. Being single-target does not add bonus damage: under the same conditions it matches Pilla’s single-target damage and covers fewer enemies.
In a chain
After a Pilla, Golla provides a different PB and keeps raising N; the two are different skills even with the same formula.

Base power Btrunc(Synchro × ATP × 0.075 + IQ × 7.5 + 1,200)

Statue Pilla

How it works
Attacks several enemies around you. Unlike Estlla’s straight line ahead, it suits a group gathered around you.
What affects it
Currently uses the same ATP formula as Golla; Synchro, IQ and MST also affect damage. The ATA formula is an unused path, and ATA plays no part in Pilla damage here.
When to use
Under the same conditions its base power is higher than Farlla’s, and it hits several targets. The total payoff depends on how many targets it hits and each one’s ELT; do not treat the single-target example as the whole blast’s total.
In a chain
Suited to area damage, amplified by Q and N. Choose the first character by ATP, Synchro and IQ; firing later yourself does not switch to your own stats.

Goddess and Twins: what the heal and support do

Neither PB deals direct damage. Used alone they affect you; chains and donations extend the effects to the participants. Being in the same room is not the same as participating.

Goddess Leilla

How it works
Casts Resta to restore HP. Leilla does not cure status effects or revive; Anti and Reverser come from the Twins below.
What affects it
Healing depends on IQ, MST, Q and N, not on Synchro, ATP or EVP. The chain multiplies the base healing, and MST × 0.5 is added afterwards.
When to use
Use it to top up participants who are low on HP; actual healing is capped by missing HP. If a teammate needs to stay at low HP, coordinate first so you do not fill it up.
In a chain
It heals and can also slot into a chain as a different PB, strengthening later effects such as the Twins; do not pick it to revive fallen teammates.

Healing · truncate after each lineB = trunc(IQ × 0.5 + 150)
R = trunc(B × Q × N × 0.01)
Healing = trunc(MST × 0.5 + R)

By the formula: IQ 200, MST 500, Q 100 heals 500 HP alone and 1,250 HP in a four-PB chain.Wiki: Leilla’s effect and healing formula.

Twins Mylla & Youlla

How it works
Casts Shifta and Deband. It starts by casting Anti on living participants and Reverser on fallen ones; players revived this time do not get this Shifta/Deband.
What affects it
The support level depends only on the first Mag’s IQ, the PB amount Q and the effective chain count N; it ignores Synchro, ATP, EVP and MST. By the formula, with IQ 200 and Q 100 without donations, a single blast gives Lv.21, two give Lv.41, three Lv.61 and four Lv.81.
When to use
Good for giving the party high-level attack and defense support, especially for the physical damage that follows. If everyone should get the buff, make sure they have joined and are alive when this Twins blast starts.
In a chain
The Twins are often the main payoff of a chain. To raise the level, check the first Mag’s IQ and the effective N first; let other PBs lead in, and avoid two Twins in a row overwriting each other.

Attack PBs: base power first, then damage

Calculate B with the formula on each card above, then S and damage in turn. trunc(x) means drop the decimals; truncate after each line before using it in the next, not only at the final damage.

S = trunc(B × Q × N × 0.01 + MST)

Damage = trunc((S ÷ 5) × ((100 − target ELT) ÷ 100))

PBs use the formulas in this section, not the class or equipment technique boosts from Section D.

Chains and donations: they change N and Q respectively

  • How to join: teammates in the same room must chain or donate before the first PB’s start-up animation ends; chaining players fire their own PB, and donors give their own PB gauge value.
  • Chaining: each valid join raises N, and the PBs fire in the order they started. When two of the same PB come back to back, the earlier one is overwritten, and the overwritten player gets no benefit from that chain. Only back-to-back repeats are limited; the Wiki’s Golla → Twins → Golla → Twins is a valid four-chain example.Wiki: chain rules.
  • Donating: raises Q; each player can donate at most 30 PB. The default keyboard key is End, donating 10 PB per press; on a gamepad it is the button above the action palette that opens Word Select/Symbol Chat. Donating also lets that participant receive the PB’s effect; donating alone does not raise N.Wiki: donation rules.
  • Different views: damage, healing and support are calculated on each client; latency or desync can make players see different orders and results. Plug in the first character, PB count and PB amount you actually observed.Wiki: client-side calculation.

H: Mag event trigger rates

Trigger events

EventCondition
100 PBWhen the PB gauge reaches 100
1/10 HPWhen you lose more than 20% HP in one frame and HP falls below 10%
Boss roomWhen entering a boss room

The Mag checks its trigger conditions once per frame. The 1/10 HP event only compares HP across two frames, regardless of where the HP loss came from, so a Berserk weapon hitting 4 or more targets, Gal Wind’s 3rd hit, or casting a technique with Psycho Wand equipped can all trigger it. When several conditions are met in the same frame only one is checked, in the order 100 PB → boss room → 1/10 HP. The death event is disabled in Blue Burst.

For event conditions and check order, see the Ephinea Wiki on Mag triggers.
