🎯 Auto-facing in combos (revised)

This revision of the original page awaits in-game verification. Amber markers identify rewritten passages; expand “Compare with the original” to read the replaced text. The original procedures and use cases are retained.

At the start of each combo attack, your character chooses a facing direction, then gradually turns towards it during that attack. This auto-facing rule determines whether you can maintain your angle throughout a combo.

🔹 Turning rules during a combo

1. First attack (1st combo step)

  • Pressing an attack button automatically turns your character towards the nearest enemy in the targeting area.
  • The area depends on the weapon's range and horizontal and vertical angles. The game reads the target list generated immediately before the button press.

2. Second / third attack (2nd / 3rd combo step)

REVISED

You only turn towards the target recorded during the previous attack. At the start, the game checks whether it is still valid (alive and targetable). If so, you turn towards it; if it is dead or invalid, you keep your current direction without acquiring a new enemy.

Compare with the original

Auto-facing occurs only when the current target is the same as the previous target.

The code does not compare targets for equality. The second and third attacks read the target saved during the previous attack's hit processing and only check whether it remains valid.

🧠 Advanced techniques: controlling or bypassing auto-facing

✅ 1. Snap aim (first attack only)

  • When you move the stick and press an attack button, your character immediately faces the input direction and turns and attacks on the spot, without moving.
REVISED

Requirement: at the moment you press the button, there must be no targetable enemy in the targeting area of your original direction. If an enemy is already in that area, the character still records it and turns back towards it during the attack.

Compare with the original

Your character attacks in the input direction without automatically targeting an enemy.

Directional input does immediately change your facing, but the first attack then reads the target list. A valid target in that list still has its angle recorded.

  • Uses:
  • Avoiding ranged attacks from Indi Belra
  • Trapping enemies against a wall, such as Ill Gill and Gibbles in the Control Tower

✅ 2. Precise multi-target kills with

With , you can deal with several targets precisely:

Sequence:

1. N1: manually aim at the gap between two enemies with low HP (avoid targeting either).

2. S2: kill target A.

3. S3: kill target B.

REVISED

📌 How it works: N1 hits nothing and records no target, so S2 does not turn automatically. S2 uses the special attack's wider targeting area to hit A. A becomes invalid as soon as the kill is determined, leaving no target to turn towards when S3 starts, so you keep your angle and shoot B.

Counterexample: if S2 misses or fails to kill A, S3 turns towards A. Projectiles themselves also travel towards their targets; hitting an enemy to the side does not require the body to turn.

Compare with the original

N1 does not acquire a target → S2/S3 do not trigger auto-facing → you maintain the precise firing angle.

An empty N1 explains only S2. Whether S3 turns depends on whether the target recorded by S2 is still valid when S3 starts, so killing A with S2 is necessary.

✅ 3. No auto-facing when there is no enemy

  • If you start the first attack (N1) before enemies spawn or enter range, automatic turning does not occur.
REVISED

This applies only to the first attack. A later attack can still turn towards a newly spawned enemy recorded during the previous attack's hit processing.

✅ 4. Palette cancel

Switch palettes to prevent an attack from being emitted or recording a target:

How to do it:

  • Press an attack button on the front palette.
  • At the same time, hold the back-palette button to switch to a palette that does not have that attack type assigned.
REVISED

The switch must take effect before the attack's emission check; otherwise it cannot cancel the attack. Cancellation depends on whether the new palette has a configuration matching this attack, not on which button you press.

Compare with the original

At the same time, hold any button on the back palette that has no attack assigned.

The code checks whether any of the 14 targeting configurations still enables this attack type after the switch. One unassigned button is not sufficient.

🔍 Effects:

  • No projectile is created.
  • No target is hit, even in melee.
  • No enemy is targeted.
  • Allied freeze traps and similar objects are not detonated (player observation, not checked in the code).

📌 Result: this combo step emits no attack event and records no target, so the next step has no target to turn towards.

Common use:

  • When enemies have just spawned, use a Shot or Needle to maintain your angle for a subsequent precise S3 kill.

Basis and limitations of this revision
  • The explanation comes from static client-code analysis. The relevant functions are byte-for-byte identical in the Haven, Tethealla and Ephinea clients. No frame-by-frame in-game validation has been performed; this page must pass in-game verification before replacing the original.
  • Ephinea applies runtime patches to damage and special-attack functions after launch. These were checked and do not change the conclusion that a kill immediately invalidates the target.
